I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in AI Security and Privacy Auditing at the University of York. My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, security and privacy: building tools and methods to audit existing and future AI systems for the harms they can cause.

At a Glance

  • Postdoctoral researcher auditing AI systems for security and privacy risks
  • PhD in machine learning for communication networks, focused on adversarial poisoning of distributed learning
  • Research spans federated learning, reinforcement learning and satellite communications
  • Background in software engineering, including a lead back-end developer role
  • Reviewer for leading AI venues and IEEE journals

Education

I hold a PhD in Machine Learning Techniques for Communication Networks from Loughborough University. My thesis critically evaluated Federated Learning and Split Learning under adversarial poisoning attacks. Key contributions include:

  • Delphi, a novel poisoning attack that induces uncertainty in the global model
  • Four attack formulations: Bayesian optimisation, least squares, reinforcement learning and multi-objective Bayesian optimisation
  • A graph-theoretic analysis of the vulnerabilities inherent in distributed learning
  • A framework for evaluating poisoning attacks against federated learning

Current Work

I work on the Participatory Harm Auditing Workbenches and Methodologies (PHAWM) project, developing auditing tools that help people examine the security and privacy behaviour of AI algorithms. My focus areas include:

  • Privacy games and attacks against foundation models
  • Reinforcement learning algorithms for alignment
  • Synthetic data generation for auditing and evaluation
  • Participatory auditing methods that involve end-users and decision subjects

MSc Supervision

I supervise MSc projects in AI privacy. Each of the four projects develops a toolkit for auditing privacy risks in a different class of model:

  • Exploring the Attack Landscape in LLMs: a toolkit for testing hypotheses about privacy risks in large language models, such as the effect of fine-tuning and model scale
  • Data Extraction from Speech Models: a toolkit for detecting whether specific voices were used to train a speech model
  • Dataset Inference and Watermarking Detection: a toolkit for detecting whether a dataset was used in training and for identifying watermarks
  • Privacy for Diffusion Models: a toolkit for assessing privacy risks in diffusion models, mainly attribute inference
