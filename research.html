My research develops methods to make AI systems auditable, secure and privacy-preserving, with a particular focus on distributed and federated settings.

  • Measuring how much AI systems leak, and how they can be attacked
  • Framing attacks and defences as games that can be audited
  • Building participatory tools so non-experts can examine AI behaviour
  • Testing the robustness of federated and split learning

Research Interests

Privacy

Understanding and mitigating privacy leakage in machine learning, from membership inference to the privacy risks of foundation models.

Privacy Games

Game-theoretic formulations of attacks and defences, framing privacy and security as adversarial interactions to be measured and audited.

AI Auditing

Participatory harm auditing workbenches and methodologies (PHAWM) for evaluating the security and privacy behaviour of existing and future AI algorithms.

Federated Learning

Robustness of federated and split learning under adversarial poisoning, and the trustworthiness of distributed and personalised learning systems.

PhD Research

My PhD, Critical Evaluation of Federated Learning and Split Learning using Adversarial Poisoning Attacks, studied how distributed learning can be undermined. Highlights:

  • Delphi, a novel poisoning attack that induces uncertainty in the global model
  • Four ways of inducing uncertainty: Bayesian optimisation, least squares, reinforcement learning and multi-objective Bayesian optimisation
  • A graph-theoretic analysis of the vulnerabilities inherent in distributed learning
  • Evaluation across federated, split and personalised federated learning

Beyond Federated Learning

My work also extends to related security and communications problems:

  • Reinforcement learning for satellite downlink precoding under delayed channel state information
  • Multi-agent reinforcement learning in multi-satellite systems
  • Adversarial machine learning attacks on modulation classification
  • Reinforcement learning for automated defence and risk-based moving target defence
  • Semantic communications for vehicle-based mission-critical services

Supervised Student Projects

Four Student projects I supervise each build a toolkit for auditing a specific kind of model:

  • Exploring the Attack Landscape in LLMs: hypotheses around fine-tuning and large models
  • Data Extraction from Speech Models: detecting whether voices were used in training
  • Dataset Inference and Watermarking Detection
  • Privacy for Diffusion Models: mainly attribute inference

Methods & Tools

  • Adversarial machine learning and poisoning attacks
  • Reinforcement learning, including multi-agent settings
  • Bayesian and convex optimisation
  • Graph analysis
  • Federated, split and personalised federated learning
  • Application areas: security, IoT and communication networks
